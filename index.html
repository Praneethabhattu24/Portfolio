Introduction

I am a Data Specialist with a Master of Science in Data Science from the University of Houston–Clear Lake, with a strong focus on advanced analytics, machine learning, and end-to-end data solutions. Through my academic and project-based work, I developed a solid foundation in Python, SQL, data modeling, statistical analysis, and data visualization, along with hands-on experience using tools such as Power BI, Tableau, and cloud-based analytics platforms. My work emphasizes transforming raw data into reliable, scalable, and decision-ready insights.

During my graduate studies, I worked as a Data Research Analyst at the University of Houston–Clear Lake, where I designed and implemented end-to-end data pipelines to support academic and research initiatives. I developed robust Python- and SQL-based workflows for data extraction, transformation, validation, and analysis, and created interactive Power BI dashboards to communicate insights and support data-driven decision-making. In addition, I gained hands-on experience with cloud and backend systems, including deploying Java-based applications on AWS EC2, working within Linux environments, and performing database validation, optimization, and routine maintenance to ensure data reliability and system integrity.

Previously, I worked as a Data Analyst at Best Agro Life Ltd., where I analyzed agricultural and field-level datasets related to crop diseases, pesticide usage, and sales performance across multiple regions. I was responsible for collecting and organizing data from field surveys, identifying trends and patterns affecting crop productivity, and creating visual reports to support business and field teams. My analyses contributed to data-driven recommendations that improved product performance assessment and supported strategic decision-making in agricultural operations.

I hold Microsoft Azure Fundamentals and Microsoft Azure Data Fundamentals certifications, reflecting a strong foundation in cloud concepts, data services, and analytics. These credentials support my ability to work with data-driven systems and reinforce my commitment to continuous learning.

Projects

Job Recommendation System

This project focuses on building a personalized job recommendation system that matches users with relevant job opportunities based on their qualifications, preferences, and career goals. The system integrates multiple datasets related to salary, job duties, education requirements, geographic location, and occupational prestige. Using natural language processing techniques, the project computes text similarity between user-provided job descriptions and job duties to generate relevant recommendations. The system also incorporates a feedback mechanism to continuously improve recommendation quality, offering a more adaptive and user-centric alternative to traditional keyword-based job platforms.

Technologies Used

Python, Pandas, NumPy, Natural Language Processing (NLP), Machine Learning, Streamlit, SQL

E-Element Genomic Annotation Project

This bioinformatics research project involves the annotation of genomic regions from Drosophila willistoni as part of the Genomics Education Partnership (GEP). The project focused on identifying gene structures, isoforms, exon–intron boundaries, and validating gene models through comparative genomics. Using evidence from RNA-Seq data, protein alignments, and gene prediction tools, I performed detailed gene model validation and prepared annotation files for potential inclusion in scientific publications and GenBank submissions. This project strengthened my skills in genomic analysis, biological data interpretation, and research documentation.

Technologies Used:

Genome Browsers (GEP UCSC), BLAST, Gene Model Checker, RNA-Seq Analysis, Comparative Genomics

Data Visualization Analysis

The findings support data-driven decision-making for sustainable agriculture and policy planning.

Crime Clearance Time Prediction-Machine Learning

This machine learning project analyzes crime data from the City of Austin to predict the time required to clear reported crime cases. Using a large real-world dataset spanning over a decade, the project involved extensive data cleaning, feature engineering, and exploratory analysis to capture temporal, spatial, and offense-related patterns. Multiple regression models—including Elastic Net, Random Forest, and K-Nearest Neighbors—were trained and evaluated using metrics such as RMSE, MAE, and R². The Random Forest model achieved the best performance, providing valuable insights into factors influencing crime resolution efficiency.

Technologies Used:

Python, Scikit-learn, Elastic Net Regression, Random Forest, KNN, Feature Engineering, Regression Modeling, Model Evaluation (RMSE, MAE, R²)

Disease Clustering - Graph Convolutional Networks

This project focuses on replicating and extending a published research paper on disease clustering using Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). The original study applied GCN-based techniques on a biological feature space of 220 features. In this work, the methodology was faithfully reproduced and further scaled to a larger dataset with 1000 features to evaluate robustness, scalability, and clustering consistency.The project involved constructing disease–gene networks using curated bioinformatics datasets, generating graph-based embeddings through GCN models, and applying clustering techniques to analyze disease similarity. Clustering quality was evaluated using distance-based metrics and silhouette analysis, demonstrating results consistent with the original research while highlighting computational and modeling trade-offs when scaling to higher-dimensional feature spaces

Technologies Used:

Python, PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), NetworkX, Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn, Bioinformatics Datasets
